What is real

Real: the wiring. Every proofread neuron of an adult female Drosophila brain, every connection of five or more synapses, the sign and strength of each one, and the integrate-and-fire dynamics from Shiu et al. The reflexes fall out of that: sugar drives MN9 and the proboscis extends, bitter cancels it, a shadow fires the giant fibre, a poke drives descending neurons. Smells go in through the real receptor neurons and come out as a sparse, smell-specific set of Kenyon cells. Learning is the fly's own rule: dopamine neurons of one mushroom-body compartment weaken the Kenyon-cell synapses onto that compartment's output neuron, reward on the avoidance side, punishment on the approach side.

Added to the model: a little spike-frequency adaptation so driven loops settle; a brain-wide activity ceiling; the antennal lobe's local neurons forced inhibitory, as they are in every real fly, where the data left their transmitter unpredicted; and a quiet resting hum in the sensory neurons so the brain is never silent.

Staged: the body. No muscle model. Motor and descending firing rates are smoothed onto a rigged low-poly fly. It walks when its descending neurons fire and turns toward the busier side; it heads up a smell's gradient when its mushroom body says like and away when it says avoid; the walk to a visible droplet, the flights, and the hunger clock are hand-written. The connectome decides what the fly does; the page decides how that looks.
