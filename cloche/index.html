What is real

Real: the wiring. Females run every proofread neuron of the FlyWire female brain; males run the Janelia male central nervous system, brain and nerve cord. Every connection of five or more synapses, its sign and strength, and the integrate-and-fire dynamics from Shiu et al. Reflexes fall out of that: sugar drives MN9 and the proboscis extends, bitter cancels it, a shadow fires the giant fibre, a poke drives descending neurons. Smells arrive through the real receptor neurons as a sparse, smell-specific set of Kenyon cells, and learning is the fly's own rule: dopamine neurons of one mushroom-body compartment weaken that compartment's Kenyon-cell synapses. Courtship runs on the male's real wiring: seeing a female drives his LC10a neurons, the P1 cluster and the pIP10 song command follow; she hears through JO-A and her vpoDN acceptance neuron answers; a mated female's oviDN neurons command each egg.

Added to the model: rate-dependent spike-frequency adaptation so driven loops settle; a slow brain-wide activity ceiling; neuromodulatory synapses scaled to a tenth; the antennal lobe's local neurons forced inhibitory where the data left their transmitter unpredicted; the male's taste neurons sorted into sugar and bitter by matching their downstream cell types to the female's; and a quiet resting hum in the sensory neurons.

Staged: the bodies. No muscle model. Firing rates are smoothed onto rigged low-poly flies. Walking to food, the flights, the hunger clock, the mounting itself, the "mated" flag and the trigger that drives oviDN on fruit are hand-written. Eggs, larvae and pupae have no brain: the larva's connectome is a different animal. The connectomes decide what the flies do; the page decides how that looks.
